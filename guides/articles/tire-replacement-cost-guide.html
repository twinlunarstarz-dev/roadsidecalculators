Tire guide

What determines the cost of replacement tires?

The price on the tire itself is only one part of the checkout total. Tire size, brand, performance category, installation package, disposal, taxes and optional protection plans can all change what you pay.

Updated September 2026Tire buying guide

Tire size and vehicle requirements

Larger wheel diameters, low-profile sizes, high load ratings, specialty EV fitments and performance tires can cost more than common passenger-car sizes. Start with the exact size and load/speed specifications listed for the vehicle rather than shopping by price tier alone.

Brand and tire category

Economy, mainstream and premium brands can have very different prices even in the same size. All-season, all-weather, winter, off-road and high-performance tires also use different compounds and construction, which affects price and intended use.

Mounting, balancing and TPMS service

Retailers may bundle mounting and balancing into an installation package or list them separately. Valve stems, TPMS service kits or sensor replacement may add cost. Ask what the installation package includes before comparing two tire quotes.

Disposal, taxes and optional coverage

Old-tire disposal fees and state or local taxes can appear at checkout. Road-hazard plans are optional at many retailers and should be compared by price, duration, exclusions and whether repair or replacement is prorated.

Repair versus replacement

A small puncture in the repairable tread area may be fixable after inspection. Damage in the sidewall or shoulder, large punctures, internal damage, excessive wear or driving on a tire while severely underinflated can make replacement necessary. Safety and manufacturer guidance should come before the cheapest option.
