Tire guide

What determines the cost of replacement tires?

The price on the tire itself is only one part of the checkout total. Tire size, brand, performance category, installation package, disposal, taxes and optional protection plans can all change what you pay.

Updated September 2026Tire buying guide

Tire size and vehicle requirements

Larger wheel diameters, low-profile sizes, high load ratings, specialty EV fitments and performance tires can cost more than common passenger-car sizes. Start with the exact size and load/speed specifications listed for the vehicle rather than shopping by price tier alone.

Brand and tire category

Economy, mainstream and premium brands can have very different prices even in the same size. All-season, all-weather, winter, off-road and high-performance tires also use different compounds and construction, which affects price and intended use.

Mounting, balancing and TPMS service

Retailers may bundle mounting and balancing into an installation package or list them separately. Valve stems, TPMS service kits or sensor replacement may add cost. Ask what the installation package includes before comparing two tire quotes.

Disposal, taxes and optional coverage

Old-tire disposal fees and state or local taxes can appear at checkout. Road-hazard plans are optional at many retailers and should be compared by price, duration, exclusions and whether repair or replacement is prorated.

Repair versus replacement

A small puncture in the repairable tread area may be fixable after inspection. Damage in the sidewall or shoulder, large punctures, internal damage, excessive wear or driving on a tire while severely underinflated can make replacement necessary. Safety and manufacturer guidance should come before the cheapest option.

Avoid unnecessary replacements and tire-shop upsells

A tire recommendation can be legitimate and still deserve verification. If a shop recommends several tires or expensive add-ons while you are stranded or under time pressure, ask for the measurements and evidence behind the recommendation before treating it as settled fact.

  • Get the exact tire on the quote. The estimate should identify brand, model, size, load index, speed rating and price per tire. At pickup, check that the tires installed match what you agreed to.
  • Ask for tread-depth measurements. If you are told two or four tires must be replaced, ask for the measured tread depth of each tire and the specific reason. AWD and some performance vehicles can legitimately require closely matched tire circumference, so check the owner's manual rather than assuming either the shop or the cheapest option is automatically right.
  • Ask to see the damage. For a puncture, sidewall problem, uneven wear or impact damage, ask the technician to show you the location and explain why repair is or is not appropriate.
  • Separate required work from optional products. Road-hazard plans, nitrogen, premium valve caps and other extras are not the same as the tires themselves. Ask whether each item is optional and what benefit it actually provides.
  • Question automatic TPMS replacement. A TPMS service kit and a complete sensor replacement are different things. If new sensors are recommended, ask which sensor failed and how it was diagnosed.
  • Do not treat an alignment as automatically mandatory with every tire purchase. Alignment can be important for uneven wear, steering pull, suspension work or impact damage, but ask what symptom or measurement supports the recommendation. If an alignment is performed, request the before-and-after printout when available.
  • Compare the installed total. A low advertised tire price can become expensive after mounting, balancing, disposal, taxes and add-ons. Compare the out-the-door total for the same tire, not just the price shown in an ad.
When to get another opinion: If a shop says a tire is unsafe, do not keep driving it just to comparison-shop. Ask for the evidence, arrange safe transportation if needed, and get a second inspection before authorizing a large or unclear purchase when practical.
