How rental reimbursement works after an accident

Rental reimbursement can help pay for a temporary vehicle while your car is being repaired after a covered loss, but the amount paid depends on the policy or claim limits that apply.

Updated September 2026Auto insurance guide

Find the daily limit

Many policies specify a maximum amount that can be reimbursed per day. If the rental costs more than that amount, the difference can remain your responsibility unless another party or coverage pays it.

Check the maximum number of covered days

Coverage can also stop after a set number of days, even if the vehicle is still being repaired. Ask the claim handler when the rental benefit begins and what event ends eligibility.

Look for a total policy cap

Some coverage is described with a daily amount and a maximum total benefit. The declarations page, endorsement or claim representative should provide the controlling limit.

Rental reimbursement is separate from vehicle damage coverage

Collision and comprehensive coverage address covered damage to the insured vehicle and may have a deductible. Rental reimbursement is a separate benefit in many policies. Protection products sold by a rental company are separate from both.

Common costs that may not be reimbursed

Upgrading to a more expensive vehicle, fuel, tolls, optional products, deposits, additional-driver charges and some taxes or fees may fall outside the benefit. Ask the insurer and rental company what is approved before pickup.

At-fault party claims can work differently

If another driver's insurer is responsible for the rental, payment rules can depend on liability, repair timing and state law rather than your own rental reimbursement endorsement. Keep receipts and confirm authorization with the claim handler.

Avoid accident-rental surprises, upsells and impersonation scams

After a crash, several businesses may be involved at once: insurer, body shop, rental company, tow yard and sometimes the other driver's insurer. Do not assume that one person's statement binds all of the others.

  • Confirm coverage directly with the insurer. Use the claim number and contact information from your policy, insurer app or official website. Do not rely only on a body shop or rental-desk employee saying "insurance will cover it."
  • Get the rental limits in writing when possible. Confirm the daily limit, maximum covered days, approved vehicle class, taxes/fees treatment, start date and what event ends the benefit. Save the email, claim message or chat transcript.
  • Ask whether each rental-counter product is optional. Damage waivers, supplemental liability, roadside products, prepaid fuel and upgrades may be useful in some situations, but they are separate purchases and may not be reimbursed. Read what you are accepting before signing or initialing.
  • Do not assume a "direct bill" means zero out of pocket. A rental company may bill the insurer up to an approved amount while leaving upgrades, over-limit days, fuel or other charges to you.
  • Track the approved end date. NAIC consumer guidance notes that rental rights can depend on the type of claim and, in a total-loss situation, may end around the time a settlement is offered. Ask the claim handler before keeping the rental longer.
  • Verify unexpected calls or payment requests. If someone contacts you claiming to be an adjuster, insurer or rental company and asks for urgent payment or sensitive financial information, end the call and contact the organization using a phone number or app you already know is legitimate.
  • Photograph the rental at pickup and return. Keep the condition report, fuel level, mileage and return receipt so later damage, fuel or late-return charges can be checked against your records.
If coverage is disputed: Ask the insurer to identify the policy provision or claim decision in writing. Keep receipts and communications. State insurance departments or regulators can explain complaint options when you cannot resolve a coverage or claim-handling dispute directly.
