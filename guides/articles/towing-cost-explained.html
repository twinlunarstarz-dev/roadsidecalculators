Towing guide

How towing costs are actually built

A tow bill is usually a combination of dispatch/hook-up, distance and any special work. Knowing which bucket a charge belongs to makes it easier to ask useful questions before the truck is sent.

1. Hook-up or dispatch

The base charge covers sending the truck, positioning it and loading the vehicle. AAA consumer guidance says uncovered towing can involve a base fee around $50–$150, though local markets and regulated rates can differ.

2. Mileage

Many providers add a per-mile amount after the base fee. AAA has cited a broad roughly $2.50–$7 per-mile range for uncovered service. Some operators instead quote a flat local-tow price.

3. Recovery or winching

If a vehicle is off-road, in a ditch, blocked, damaged or difficult to load, recovery labor and extra equipment can cost more than the basic tow itself. Ask whether the quote is for a normal tow or recovery.

4. Storage, tolls and impound-related charges

Storage is separate from transportation. If a vehicle is taken to a yard, ask when storage starts, whether there is a daily rate, what forms of payment are accepted and what is required for release.

5. After-hours and unusual vehicles

Night, holiday and peak-demand calls may cost more. Large trucks, RVs, damaged vehicles and certain AWD/EV configurations may need different equipment. Follow the vehicle manufacturer’s towing procedure rather than assuming one method is safe for every car.

Questions to ask before dispatch

  • What is the total hook-up/dispatch fee?
  • What is the per-mile rate and how is mileage measured?
  • Is this quote for towing only, or does it include recovery/winching?
  • Are after-hours, toll, storage or administrative fees expected?
  • What is the destination, and can I choose a repair shop?
  • Can you text or email the price before dispatch?
