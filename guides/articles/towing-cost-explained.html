Towing guide

How towing costs are built

A towing bill usually combines a base dispatch or hook-up fee with distance and any special work required to move the vehicle safely. Understanding each part of the quote makes it easier to compare providers and spot charges that need an explanation.

Updated September 2026Roadside pricing guide

Hook-up or dispatch fee

The base charge covers sending the truck, positioning it and loading the vehicle. Consumer guidance from AAA describes uncovered towing as potentially including a base fee in roughly the $50–$150 range, but local markets, regulations, equipment and service conditions can move prices outside that range.

Mileage charges

Many towing companies add a per-mile charge after the base fee. AAA has cited a broad uncovered-service range of roughly $2.50–$7 per mile. Some providers instead quote a flat local-tow price, so ask exactly how mileage is calculated before dispatch.

Recovery and winching

A standard tow assumes the vehicle is accessible and can be loaded normally. If the vehicle is off-road, in a ditch, blocked, damaged, rolled or difficult to reach, recovery labor and specialized equipment may be priced separately.

Storage and impound charges

Transportation and storage are different charges. If the vehicle is going to a tow yard, ask when storage begins, the daily rate, any release or administrative fee, accepted payment methods and the documents needed to retrieve the vehicle.

After-hours and specialty equipment

Night, holiday and high-demand calls may cost more. Heavy vehicles, RVs, damaged cars and some AWD or EV configurations can require different equipment. The vehicle owner's manual should determine the acceptable towing method.

Questions to ask before the truck is sent

  • What is the dispatch or hook-up fee?
  • What is the per-mile rate, and from where is mileage measured?
  • Does the quote include winching or recovery if needed?
  • Are after-hours, toll, storage or administrative fees expected?
  • Where will the vehicle be taken, and can I choose the destination?
  • Can the quoted price be sent by text or email?

Avoid towing scams and surprise-fee traps

Breakdowns and crashes create urgency, which makes it easier to agree to something you would normally question. Treat this guide and the calculator as a price-checking tool, not permission to accept the first truck or quote you encounter.

  • Verify who dispatched the truck. If a tow truck appears at a crash or breakdown before you requested one, confirm whether it was sent by police, your insurer, roadside-assistance provider or someone you authorized. Do not assume an unsolicited truck represents any of them.
  • Get the important charges in writing before a consensual tow. Ask for the hook-up fee, mileage rate, after-hours charge, expected recovery fee, tolls, storage rate, release/admin fees and destination. A very low phone quote that leaves the major add-ons undefined is not a useful quote.
  • Know where the vehicle is going. For a tow you voluntarily arrange, confirm the destination before loading and do not agree to a last-minute change you do not understand. Police-ordered, impound and private-property tows can follow different local rules.
  • Do not sign blank forms or vague authorizations. Read what you are authorizing, keep a copy or photo, and photograph the vehicle before loading when it is safe to do so. Record the towing company's name, truck information and destination.
  • Watch for payment pressure. Demands for gift cards, cryptocurrency, wire transfers or other hard-to-reverse payment methods are major scam signals. If a provider refuses to identify the business, explain the bill or provide an itemized receipt, document the situation before paying when possible.
  • Compare when safety allows. If the vehicle is in a safe location and the quote seems unusually high or unclear, call another towing company, your insurer or roadside club before authorizing service. At an unsafe crash scene, personal safety comes first.
If the final bill changes: Ask for an itemized invoice showing exactly what changed from the quote. Keep texts, photos, receipts and names. Towing rules are often state or local, so complaints may belong with a state attorney general, local consumer-protection office, towing regulator or law-enforcement agency.
