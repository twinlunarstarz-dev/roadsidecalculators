How to calculate EV charging cost

EV charging cost depends on how much energy the vehicle needs and what you pay for each kilowatt-hour. A useful estimate also accounts for the difference between energy stored in the battery and energy drawn from the grid.

Updated September 2026EV ownership guide

Start with the change in state of charge

If a 60 kWh usable battery moves from 20% to 80%, the battery gains 60% of its usable capacity, or about 36 kWh. That is the energy stored in the battery, not necessarily the total energy purchased from the charger.

Account for charging losses

Charging equipment and the vehicle's onboard systems are not perfectly efficient. Some energy becomes heat or is used by supporting systems, so energy drawn from the grid can be higher than the energy added to the battery. Temperature, power level and vehicle design can affect losses.

Multiply grid energy by the price per kWh

At home, use the electricity rate that actually applies to charging, including time-of-use rates when relevant. At public stations, use the posted price or membership price. Some stations also charge session, parking or idle fees.

Calculate driving cost per 100 miles

EV efficiency is commonly shown in kWh per 100 miles. If the vehicle uses 30 kWh/100 miles and electricity costs $0.20/kWh, the simple energy cost is about $6 per 100 miles. Weather, speed, HVAC use, terrain and towing can change real-world consumption.

Home versus public charging

Home charging often uses a residential electricity rate, while public fast charging may carry a higher energy price in exchange for much faster charging. The cheapest option depends on local rates, memberships and when or where you charge.
