EV charging

EV charging cost calculator

The old version used an incorrect cost-per-100-miles equation. This version separates a charging session from driving efficiency and accounts for charging losses when estimating energy drawn from the grid.

90% is a planning default. Actual losses depend on charger, temperature and vehicle.
Use your EPA label or recent trip average when available.

How the math works

Battery energy added = capacity × change in state of charge. Estimated grid energy = battery energy added ÷ charging efficiency. Session cost = grid energy × price + session fees. For driving cost, EPA-style kWh/100 mi can be multiplied directly by the electricity price when that consumption figure already represents energy drawn for vehicle use.

U.S. Department of Energy materials note that EV consumption is commonly expressed as kWh per 100 miles and that charging systems have conversion losses; DOE guidance has cited on-mode EVSE efficiency levels around 90%–97%, while older wall-to-battery studies found larger total charging losses.
